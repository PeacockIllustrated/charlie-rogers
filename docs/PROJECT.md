# Project context

## The book this site is built around

*Charlie Rogers, Pursued by Bulldozers*. Littlecroft Publishing, 2025. ISBN 978-1-7393198-4-7. 123 pages, A4. Compiled by Brian Rankin (Come View My Art Gallery, Sheriffs Highway, Low Fell). Edited by Annie Doyle and Alice Venus. Designed by BDI (Rob Barnes). Foreword by Michael Chaplin. Tribute essays from Tom Pickard (1973) and David Whetstone (1988). A 50-year memoir from collector Dennis Donnelly. All artwork copyright Charles Rogers Junior.

The PDF lives at `/pdf/charlie-rogers-book.pdf`. See `IMAGE-EXTRACTION.md` for how to pull assets from it.

## Charlie Rogers, the man

Charles Henry Rogers, born 16 January 1930 at 239 Westbourne Avenue, Gateshead. Died 27 April 2020 at Aspen Court Care Home, Gateshead, one of eleven residents lost in the first wave of Covid. Buried with his wife Ann at Saltwell Cemetery.

Evacuated to Bishop Auckland 1939, returned 1944. RAF national service from 1948. Worked variously as a clerk, Post Office staff, Rediffusion TV engineer, Barkers Electrician, briefly a Kibblesworth miner, on the docks at British Ropes, and most importantly for about a decade as a groundsman for Durham County Council. Butlins Red Coat at Filey and Clacton in the late 1950s, photographed in a football team alongside Sean Connery and Des O'Connor. Made redundant 1971, full-time artist from 1974.

Footballer first, painter second. Trialled for Sunderland in 1950, played left back for Kibblesworth Colliery Welfare into his mid-thirties. Boxed for the British Army. Supported Newcastle and Gateshead.

Married Ann Henderson (a librarian from Haltwhistle) in 1973, met at the Mayfair Ballroom. Honeymoon in Dewsbury and Manchester. Son Charlie Junior born 1974. Ann died 2015.

## The origin story

This is the hook the whole site should hang on. In 1964, aged 34, Charlie took a heavy blow to an already-damaged left knee in a cup tie for Kibblesworth Colliery Welfare. His GP signed him off for a week. Limping down Bensham Road to visit his Aunt Violet Gwendoline Woodhead at 262 Bensham Road, he sat in her front room with a cup of tea and looked out at the back lane opposite. Over five or six mornings he painted it in pen and wash. He titled the result *Back Cotfield Street*. Cotfield Street was demolished soon after.

Forty-two years later he wrote: "and all this I owe to that anonymous dirty little half-back who temporarily crippled me 42 years ago".

## The title and the thesis

"I feel I have spent much of my career being pursued by bulldozers." Charlie's own line, and the book's organising idea. He painted Tyneside's pubs, terraces, corner shops, churches, archways, and back lanes immediately before they came down. The 1960s and 70s saw vast swathes of Victorian and Edwardian housing flattened across Gateshead and Newcastle. Charlie raced the demolition teams. The local photographer Trevor Ermel was doing the same with a camera, and his black-and-white shots are placed throughout the book alongside Charlie's paintings of the same scenes.

This thesis is the soul of the site. Every painting on a demolished location should be marked as such. Where Trevor Ermel photographs exist for the same scene, they should be paired with the painting.

## The cast

People who recur and deserve their own pages or prominent mentions:

- **Ann Rogers** (1942 to 2015), wife, librarian.
- **Charlie Rogers Junior**, son, copyright holder. The book exists because Brian Rankin knocked on his door on 4 February 2023.
- **Pop** (Francis Rogers), Charlie's father. Painted asleep, reading, drinking. A bottle of Newcastle Brown Ale is Pop's signature object.
- **Norman Cornish MBE** (1919 to 2014), Spennymoor artist. Met at Charlie's first exhibition in 1965, lifelong friendship.
- **L S Lowry**, never met but the book positions Charlie alongside.
- **Dennis Donnelly**, the insurance man from Birtley. Bought about 100 paintings for himself and roughly 100 more on behalf of others between 1988 and 2018. Curated the final exhibition at Low Fell Library in 2016.
- **Aunt Violet Gwendoline Woodhead** at 262 Bensham Road, whose front window started it all.
- **Bruce**, Charlie's black dog as a young man, who reappears across the catalogue as a memorial.
- **Harry Lord**, Univision Gallery, Bigg Market, gave Charlie his first show in March 1965.
- **Trevor Ermel**, local photographer documenting the same demolitions in black and white.
- **Tom Pickard**, Newcastle poet, wrote the 1973 tribute essay.
- **Michael Chaplin**, writer (son of Sid Chaplin), wrote the foreword.
- **Brian Rankin**, compiler of the book, the reason the project exists.

## The geography

The site is geographically structured as much as biographically. Three concentric circles.

**Gateshead** is the heart. Saltwell Park (Saltwell Towers, the bandstand, the cemetery), Bensham (Bensham Road, Cotfield Street, St Cuthbert's Church, Bensham Court, Bensham Grove, Rectory Road, Westbourne Avenue, Windsor Avenue, Coatsworth Road), the Shipley Art Gallery, the Cenotaph at Shipcote, Lady Vernon School Yard, Pot-Pie Bob's on Wellington Street, Redheugh Crossroads, Dead Man's Arch, Windmill Hills, Low Fell.

**Newcastle** across the river. Bigg Market, Grey Street, Theatre Royal, Grainger Market, the Quayside (Sunday Market, fish market), the Side, High Level Bridge, Tyne Bridge, Mosley Street, Haymarket, the Castle, Moot Hall, Monument, Shephards department store.

**Beyond Tyneside**. Paris (his favourite city, over ten visits from 1964 onwards), London (Royal Academy four times, Princess Margaret), Jersey, Spennymoor, Whitley Bay, South Shields, Tynemouth, Durham, Harrogate, York, Knaresborough, Alnwick, Askrigg, Weardale.

## Style, method, signatures

Charlie's recurring visual elements, useful for tagging the catalogue and for thematic filtering on the site:

- Leaning "drunken" lampposts
- The black dog (Bruce, memorial)
- A bottle of Newcastle Brown Ale beside Pop
- Impressed CHR initials on watercolours
- White football goalposts on gable-end walls
- Snow scenes in blue, grey, lilac, yellow ochre
- Occasional self-insertion into the scene
- Pen and wash, oil, watercolour. No easel, ever.

Influences he named: Van Gogh, Modigliani, Albert Marquet, Cornish, Tom McGuinness, Lowry, Joseph Kirsop, Feliks Topolski.

## The body of work

Around 1,000 paintings and drawings across 56 years. The PDF reproduces a representative selection. Categorisation for the site's filtering:

- Streets, back lanes, demolition scenes
- Pubs and bars
- Churches
- Industrial and quayside scenes, boats, bridges
- The Quayside Sunday Market
- Family (Pop, Ann, Charlie Junior, Bruce)
- Self-portraits
- Domestic interiors
- Tyneside characters (rag-and-bone men, newspaper sellers, flower sellers, tramps, jugglers, pavement artists)
- Bar scenes and conversations
- Football matches and spectators
- Saltwell Park brass bands and Orthodox Jewish community scenes
- Paris and London street scenes
- Snow scenes and nocturnes

His final painting, half-completed at his death, was the view from his Aspen Court Care Home bedroom window across the rooftops of St Vincent Street.

## Exhibition history

Over 50 lifetime exhibitions. Highlights:

- Royal Academy Summer Exhibition: 1967, 1972, 1975, 1981
- Royal Society of British Artists, London 1967
- Scottish Royal Academy, Edinburgh 1968
- Portal Gallery, London 1970
- Salford Art Gallery 1974
- Royal Charity Exhibition 1994, opened by Princess Margaret
- Introduced to Prince Charles at the Midsummer Eve Soirée 1981
- Final lifetime exhibition: Low Fell Library, 2016 to 2017, dedicated to Ann
- Posthumous: *The World of Charlie Rogers* at Come View My Art Gallery from 2023
- *Blackwell Lane, Gateshead 1981* selected for the *Belonging* exhibition at the Laing Art Gallery in 2024

## What Brian Rankin is trying to do beyond the book

Three explicit ambitions in the text, all of which the website should reinforce:

1. A Charlie Rogers trail around Saltwell Park, in discussion with Gateshead Council.
2. A permanent exhibition at Saltwell Towers (former home of stained-glass manufacturer William Wailes).
3. A retrospective at the Shipley Gallery.

The book ends with a stated next research phase: cataloguing the contents of Charlie's surviving sketchbooks. The website is part of the wider campaign to install Charlie as the third name alongside Lowry and Cornish.

## Pull quotes worth using

"Van Gogh had his sunflowers; Monet, sunlit pools of water lilies, and Charlie Rogers has the back streets of Gateshead." Charles Westerberg.

"Rogers is one of the outstanding artists of the North East School. His highly individual work is sometimes reminiscent of Lowry, sometimes of Cornish, Buffet or Van Gogh but imitating none of these artists." Derek Kirkup, Percy Gallery 1973.

"The subjects of his paintings are the things and places you never notice until they are gone." Derek Kirkup.

"As fast as Tyneside's characteristic buildings fall Charlie Rogers seems to be there to catch them." Tom Pickard, 1973.
